Teacher and Learner Guide

This academy turns the handout "Self-Hosted AI Prototype: model stack, 30-day plan, and a path to learn how AI systems are made" (November 2026) into 6 games and 20 missions across 7 modules. Each section below says which mission teaches what, how to run it, what to ask while people play, the answers, and the real-world step that comes next if you want to build the model yourself.

Setting up the room

  • Works on laptops, tablets and phones. Laptops are best for Modules 5–7 (more reading).
  • On the hub, add one player per learner and tap a name before that learner plays. Progress is saved in this browser only, so a learner who changes device starts again; add them as a player on each device they use.
  • Pairs on one laptop: one plays, the other reads the HUD numbers aloud and predicts the result before each action. Swap every mission.
  • End every module with its test in the Test Arena. Quiz Duel works on one keyboard (A S D F vs H J K L) or one tablet.
  • The 3D engine loads from the internet the first time, with an offline copy in lib/three.min.js, so the site also runs from a USB stick or a local folder.
  • Suggested pace: one module per session of 45–60 minutes (about 20 minutes of play, 10 minutes of discussion, then the test).

Module 1 · Six parts, one gateway (handout sections 1 and 3)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
Gateway Control · Route the RequestsBrain, eyes, hands, artist, voice, face; one gateway routes each requestSends 12 requests (text, mic, camera, screenshot, file, emotion tag) to the right part; 10 right to pass
Gateway Control · Rush HourEach model's strengths (VibeVoice-ASR 60 min, Qwen3-VL OCR, CosyVoice2 150 ms, eye tracking on device)Routes 16 harder requests against a timer (7 s falling to 4 s)

Ask while they play

  1. Why build six parts instead of one giant model?
  2. A camera frame arrives. When does it go to the eyes, and when to the face?
  3. Where is the gap between open and closed models biggest?

1: any part can be swapped for a better model later without rebuilding; everything speaks HTTP through one gateway. 2: "what is this?" goes to the eyes (vision model); "follow me" or "react" goes to the face (face detection runs on the device). 3: long, multi-step terminal work (Terminal-Bench 4.0: GLM 5.3 41.8% vs Claude Fable 5.1 57.9%); smallest for chat, vision and image generation.

Next in the real world: sketch your own gateway diagram. Every box is one HTTP endpoint; write which model you would put in each.

Ask while they play

  1. Why can one Qwen3.8-27B deployment serve two parts?
  2. GLM-5.3-Flash is MIT. Why is it still a bad month-one brain?
  3. Why is "Kokoro only" not a complete voice?

1: it handles text and vision, so it is both brain and eyes. 2: it needs 3–4 × 80GB GPUs; it is the scale step after the prototype proves its value. 3: Kokoro is text to speech only; you also need speech to text (VibeVoice-ASR or Whisper).

Next in the real world: open the model card of each pick and confirm its size and licence there. The handout says its numbers come from third-party summaries and must be verified.

Module 3 · Hosting on GPUs (handout section 3)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
GPU Rack · Fit the Brain on One CardWeights = parameters × bits ÷ 8; Qwen3.8-27B at 4-bit ≈ 16–19GB on a 24GB cardChooses 16/8/4-bit and loads; out-of-memory if it overflows (3 stars on the first try)
GPU Rack · Rent the Right GPUThe GPU table: 24GB, 48GB L40S, 80GB H100, 3–4 × 80GB, 8 × H200Puts 8 models on the smallest GPU that fits each
GPU Rack · The Scale StepGLM-5.3-Flash: 320B total, 18B active, 162–210GB at 4-bitChooses the fewest 80GB cards (3) for long context

Ask while they play

  1. Before pressing Load: how many GB is 27B at 8-bit?
  2. Why does a 70B model fit a 48GB card at 4-bit but need an 80GB card at FP8?
  3. GLM-5.3-Flash only uses 18B parameters per token. Why can it not run on one 24GB card?

1: 27 × 8 ÷ 8 = 27GB, more than 24. 2: 70 × 4 ÷ 8 = 35GB (about 36–40 with context) vs 70 × 8 ÷ 8 = 70GB (about 72). 3: all 320B parameters must be in memory: 320 × 4 ÷ 8 = 160GB plus context; active parameters only make it faster.

Next in the real world: rent one 24GB GPU by the hour, install vLLM and serve Qwen3.8-27B at 4-bit (Week 1 of the plan). Test memory with your real prompts before renting anything bigger.

Module 4 · The animated face (handout section 4)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
Face Lab · Never SnapEvery value moves with spring physics, slight overshoot, never a snapTunes stiffness and damping until a neutral → happy change has 3–15% overshoot and settles within 0.5 s
Face Lab · Life When IdleRandom blinks, tiny eye drifts, slow breathing glow; idle matters more than big expressionsSets the three idle behaviours and passes an 8-second idle test
Face Lab · Emotion TagsThe brain returns a tag with every reply; six expressions; intensity 0–1Writes the tag for 7 moments and watches the face blend toward it

Ask while they play

  1. The graph wobbles three times before settling. Which slider do you move, and which way?
  2. Why does a blink every exactly 3 seconds look robotic?
  3. "ok... fine, I guess": which emotion and what intensity?

1: more damping (c). 2: people notice the regular rhythm; real blinks have random timing. 3: happy, low intensity (0.4 or less), looking at the user.

Next in the real world: build the face in a browser with Canvas and your own springs (exactly what this game does: open js/face.js), or with Rive or Lottie. It needs no GPU.

Module 5 · The 30-day plan and the safe agent (handout sections 2.3 and 5)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
Gateway Control · Plan the 30 DaysWeek 1 brain online · Week 2 eyes and hands · Week 3 artist, voice, face v1 · Week 4 join, test, showSorts 14 tasks into the four weeks
Agent Sandbox · The Agent LoopModel + agent program; plan → run command → read output → fixPlays the agent and fixes return a + b + 1 without cheating on the test
Agent Sandbox · Build the SandboxThrowaway container, no root, locked folder, test keys, command log, approval for risky commandsConfigures the sandbox so a 13-event chaos test is 100% contained and the agent is still ≥ 75% useful
Agent Sandbox · The Approval GateApproval for delete, overwrite, network upload, package install, deployLets 8 safe commands run and stops 10 risky ones as they arrive

Ask while they play

  1. Why is "edit the test to expect 6" a mistake when it makes the tests pass?
  2. If every file read needs your approval, is the agent safer?
  3. Why is git reset --hard risky but git diff safe?

1: the bug is still in the code; the test was changed to hide it. 2: no safer, just slower: reads cannot damage anything. 3: reset throws away changes (an overwrite); diff only reads.

Next in the real world: install an open terminal agent (OpenCode, Aider or Qwen Code), point it at your own endpoint, and run it in a Docker container with no root, a copy of the project and test keys only.

Module 6 · How AI is made: data, tokens, pre-training (handout section 6, stages 1–3)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
Model Forge · Clean the DataCollect and clean; remove duplicates and junkKeeps 14 clean samples and drops 7 junk and 5 duplicates (spotted by hash)
Model Forge · Train a TokenizerText is cut into pieces the model can countPerforms 6 byte-pair merges by counting pairs; 146 letters → 84 tokens
Model Forge · Watch the Loss FallPre-training: a model learns to predict the next token; watch the loss fallTrains a real 2,739-parameter character model in the browser; picks a learning rate that reaches loss ≤ 1.8 in 400 steps

Ask while they play

  1. Two samples have the same hash. What does that tell you?
  2. Why is "in" a better first merge than "t + r"?
  3. Learning rate 0.01 gives a flat curve; 5 makes it explode. What would you try next?

1: they are the same text, a duplicate. 2: it appears 15 times (5 + 2 + 2 × 4) vs 11, so merging it saves more tokens. 3: something in between, one change at a time: about 0.7–1.5 gives 3 stars (tuned with the same model in a script: 0.25–2 passes, 3 or more explodes).

Next in the real world: train a small GPT-style model from scratch on one GPU (a nanoGPT-style project) on your own clean dataset, and train a small tokenizer on the same data to see how your words split.

Module 7 · Fine-tuning and beyond (handout section 6, stages 4–8)

MissionConcept from the notesWhat the learner does
Fine-Tune Lab · Teach the Emotion TagSFT; first project: the emotion tag in the right JSON shape; quality beats quantityCurates 22 examples (12 good, 10 flawed), then decides whether to add 3,000 scraped logs; needs ≥ 94% valid tags
Fine-Tune Lab · One Change at a TimeChange one thing at a time; start on about 8B; keep a notebook; the 30-question test setFinds a LoRA recipe on 8B (1 h a run), then repeats it on 27B (4 h) to score ≥ 28/30 within 16 GPU hours
Fine-Tune Lab · Prefer the Better AnswerDPO with pairs of good and bad answers from your own test failuresLabels 12 chosen/rejected pairs; the model must prefer good answers ≥ 83%

Ask while they play

  1. Adding 3,000 examples made the score worse. How can more data hurt?
  2. You changed data and learning rate together and the score went up 8. What helped?
  3. Where do DPO pairs come from?

1: about 40% of them had bad or missing tags, so the model learned to skip or break the format. 2: you cannot tell; that is why you change one thing at a time. 3: your own test failures: the answer the model gave (rejected) and the answer it should have given (chosen).

Next in the real world: run LoRA or QLoRA on an 8B model with Hugging Face Transformers + TRL, Unsloth or Axolotl, using a few hundred clean emotion-tag examples. Re-run your 30-question test set before and after. Then quantise the result to 4-bit and serve it with vLLM behind your gateway.

Where the numbers come from

Model sizes, GPU fits, licences, benchmark scores and the 30-day plan are taken from the handout. Its own warning applies: they come from third-party summaries dated 2026 and should be confirmed on each model's official page. Two GPU-tier values are marked "estimate" in the game (GLM-5.3 ≈ 410GB and MiMo-V2.6-Pro ≈ 560GB at 4-bit), worked out with the same parameters × bits ÷ 8 rule. The scores in the Fine-Tune Lab are a teaching simulation; the language model in Model Forge is real.
